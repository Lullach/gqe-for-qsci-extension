# Research Plan

## 1. Title

Transferable Generative Quantum Circuit Design for Quantum-Selected Configuration Interaction: Generalizing a Generative Quantum Eigensolver across Molecular Hamiltonians

## 2. Background

Quantum-selected configuration interaction (QSCI) estimates molecular ground-state energies by sampling electronic configurations from a quantum circuit and diagonalizing the Hamiltonian classically within the sampled subspace. Its accuracy depends on the circuit, which must concentrate probability on the configurations that dominate the ground state. The generative quantum eigensolver (GQE) addresses this design problem by training a generative model, originally a GPT-2 transformer, to compose circuits from a pool of excitation operators, using the resulting QSCI energy as the reward. GQE-QSCI has reached chemical accuracy on strongly correlated systems such as stretched N2. However, the model must be trained from scratch for every molecule. Operators are represented as integer indices into a molecule-specific pool, so a trained model cannot be applied to another system, and the training cost, dominated by repeated quantum sampling and diagonalization, is paid again for every new geometry or molecule. Machine-learning models in chemistry commonly transfer between molecules by describing their inputs through physical features rather than identities; to our knowledge, this idea has not yet been applied to generative circuit design.

## 3. Purpose

This research aims to make generative circuit design for QSCI transferable, so that a single model trained on a set of molecules generates useful circuits for molecules it has never seen. Three levels of generalization of increasing difficulty are studied: different geometries of the same molecule (N2 bond dissociation); different molecules with different atoms, operator sets and qubit counts (training on LiH and H2O, evaluating on N2); and systems larger than any seen in training (training on H4, H6 and H8 hydrogen chains, evaluating on H10). The central question is how much of the accuracy of a model trained directly on the target system can be recovered without any training on it (zero-shot) or with little additional training (few-shot). A second aim is to determine which generative architecture transfers best, comparing transformer, diffusion and graph neural network policies, and to benchmark the generated circuits against classical selected configuration interaction at equal subspace size. The intended contribution is a reusable circuit-design method that reduces the per-molecule training cost of QSCI, rather than a claim of quantum advantage.

## 4. Methods

Operators are described by physical features instead of indices: orbital energies and gaps, coupled-cluster and MP2 amplitudes, Hartree–Fock couplings, qubit footprint and gate cost. A shared scoring network compares the generator's internal state with these feature vectors, so the same parameters define a distribution over any molecule's operator set. This scorer is implemented in five policies: GPT-2, single-shot and masked diffusion, a graph-denoiser diffusion model, and a directed-acyclic-graph neural network representing circuits as gate-dependency graphs with commutation-aware canonical ordering. Policies are trained with group relative policy optimization on the QSCI energy; for diffusion models, the exact denoising-trajectory likelihood replaces the variational bound. Multi-molecule training cycles through the training set using fixed global feature normalization, and held-out molecules are evaluated without gradient updates. Each experiment is compared against an equal-budget model trained on the target, reporting errors relative to CASCI over several random seeds. A pointer-based action space, constructing excitations directly from orbitals, removes the dependence on a coupled-cluster-screened pool. Circuits are simulated with CUDA-Q on the ABCI-Q GPU cluster.

## 5. Progress of Research

Five generative policies, including a graph-based model and exact-likelihood diffusion training, are implemented in a common framework, and feature-based operator scoring has been added to all of them; on N2 it matches the original index-based model, indicating that the transferable representation does not reduce accuracy. The multi-molecule training loop, zero-shot evaluation and matched baselines are complete, as is a classical heat-bath configuration interaction reference. Commutation-aware canonical ordering and operator-pool deduplication were evaluated and showed no significant effect. The software environment on the ABCI-Q cluster has been validated and the experiments now run there routinely.

The principal experiment is size extrapolation on hydrogen chains: one policy is trained on H4, H6 and H8 at nine bond lengths, twenty-seven systems in total spanning eight to sixteen qubits, and evaluated without further training on H10, which uses twenty qubits and is therefore larger than anything seen during training. Both the transferred policy and a matched baseline trained directly on H10 have been run over five random seeds. An earlier experiment that trained on lithium hydride and water and evaluated on nitrogen has been retired: two training systems are too few to constitute a training set, and it used a fixed subspace cap whose coverage varied by two orders of magnitude across the set, so the reward was not comparable between systems. Transfer between chemically distinct molecules will be revisited with a substantially larger and more diverse training set.

Comparison against the classical reference established the appropriate measure of success. On H10 chemical accuracy requires between thirty and fifty per cent of the full configuration space, so no selected configuration interaction method attains it at a practical subspace size, and reporting against chemical accuracy would invite enlarging the subspace until a method that needs half of full configuration interaction remains. The meaningful comparison is instead the energy error at equal subspace size. Measured this way the policy trained on the target is more accurate than heat-bath configuration interaction and far more accurate than random determinant selection, and the transferred policy retains part of that advantage. These margins are currently obtained by interpolating the classical curve and require an exact evaluation at matched subspace size before they are reported as results.

The remaining work is that exact matched-size comparison, the architecture comparison across the five policies, few-shot fine-tuning on the held-out system, evaluation of a pointer action space that constructs excitations directly from orbitals and removes the dependence on a coupled-cluster-screened pool, which is implemented and validated but not yet run as an experiment, and writing the thesis. The number of distinct configurations obtained per circuit appears to be a structural limitation of the approach as a whole and is being examined separately.
